The short version

Learning is
a process.

Your brain strengthens useful pathways when you actively work with information. That means the best study session is not the one that feels easiest—it is the one that asks you to think, connect, and return.

Three moving parts

How learning
takes hold.

Use this mental model to turn a vague goal like “study biology” into actions your brain can actually practice.

01

Attention

Information cannot become a memory if you never really notice it. Reduce distractions and work in a clear, short window.

Notice it
02

Encoding

Connect new ideas to what you already know. Explain a concept in your own words and create a useful example.

Connect it
03

Retrieval

Pulling an idea from memory makes the pathway stronger. Close the book and see what you can reconstruct.

A better study loop

Plan for
the long game.

Use a repeatable cycle instead of waiting for motivation. The goal is steady contact with the material over time.

  1. 01

    Preview / 5 minutes

    Scan headings and write two questions you want the session to answer.

  2. 02

    Learn / 20 minutes

    Read, watch, or listen with a pen in hand. Translate important ideas into your own language.

  3. 03

    Recall / 10 minutes

    Put the resources away. Draw, explain, or answer from memory.

  4. 04

    Return / tomorrow

    Revisit the same ideas after a night of sleep, then add a little more distance.
