The short version

From check-in
to carousel.

A baggage handling system, or BHS, is the airport’s behind-the-scenes logistics network. It identifies each bag, routes it to the right flight, and reunites it with the passenger at the other end.

See the moving parts ↓

The passenger view

Six handoffs
behind one trip.

The exact equipment and sequence vary by airport, but the journey usually follows the same logic: identify, inspect, direct, and deliver.

Current stop 01 / 06

Check-in

The airline prints a bag tag with a unique identifier and routing information. The tag helps the system know where this bag needs to go.

The barcode is the bag’s travel passport.

The system behind the scene

Logic, motion,
coordination.

A BHS combines physical infrastructure with data. Every scan is a small confirmation that the physical bag and its digital record still agree.

The final moment

Goodbye,
then hello.

At the destination, the direction reverses. Bags leave the aircraft, enter the arrivals system, and travel to the carousel where a passenger can take over again.
